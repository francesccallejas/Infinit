Refresh or rebrand? How to know how far to go

Not every brand needs to start from zero. How to tell whether you need a refresh, a repositioning or a full rebrand, and what each one changes and keeps.

Cesc CallejasFounder, INFINIT©6 October 2026 · 2 min read
In this articleThree levelsWhat to keepSigns it’s more than a refreshThe two mistakesHow we decideFAQ
Short answerIf people understand what you do but the brand looks dated, a refresh is enough. If it looks fine but says the wrong thing, because you have grown, changed markets or added services, you need to reposition. If both are true, or after a merger, a new investor or a change of generation, it is a full rebrand. The strategy tells you which.

“Rebrand” sounds like starting over. Most of the time it isn’t. The real question is how far to go, and the answer depends on what still works.

Three levels

RefreshRepositioningRebrand
What changesVisual system, type, colour, applicationsStory, messages, website, sales materialStrategy, identity, applications, and the name if needed
What staysName, the essence of the logo, the messageName and logo, often refreshedWhatever the strategy says is worth keeping
Typical triggerA dated lookNew services, markets or clientsMerger, acquisition, new owner, new generation
Guide timingFrom 2 weeksStrategy in 2–4 weeks, then the websiteStrategy and brand in little over a month, then the website

What to keep

Decades of trust are an asset. Before changing anything, ask clients, sales and the team what they would miss: the name, a colour, a symbol, a phrase. What people recognise is worth protecting. What nobody notices can go.

Signs it’s more than a refresh

  • The company has gone from local to international.
  • A new business line doesn’t fit the current story.
  • There has been a merger, an acquisition or a new investor.
  • Each salesperson explains the company differently.
  • The website describes a company that no longer exists.

The two mistakes

Doing too little. A new logo on top of an unclear story. The confusion stays, only better dressed.

Doing too much. Throwing away recognition you spent decades building, for the sake of looking new.

How we decide

We start with a short strategy: interviews with management, sales and key clients, one workshop and a deliverable session. At the end you know what to keep, what to change and in what order, and whether you need a refresh or a rebrand.

FAQ

Can we refresh now and rebrand later?

Yes, if the strategy is clear. A refresh without strategy usually ends up being redone.

What if the founders want to keep everything?

That’s common in family businesses. The strategy listens to everyone — founders, the next generation and the team — and decisions are taken together.
