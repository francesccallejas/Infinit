# Refresh or rebrand? How to know how far to go

**Short answer:** if people understand what you do but the brand looks dated, a refresh is enough. If it looks fine but says the wrong thing, because you have grown, changed markets or added services, you need to reposition. If both are true, or after a merger, a new investor or a change of generation, it is a full rebrand. The strategy tells you which.

“Rebrand” sounds like starting over. Most of the time it isn’t. The real question is how far to go, and the answer depends on what still works.

## Three levels

| | Refresh | Repositioning | Rebrand |
|---|---|---|---|
| What changes | Visual system, type, colour, applications | Story, messages, website, sales material | Strategy, identity, applications, and the name if needed |
| What stays | Name, the essence of the logo, the message | Name and logo, often refreshed | Whatever the strategy says is worth keeping |
| Typical trigger | A dated look | New services, markets or clients | Merger, acquisition, new owner, new generation |
| Guide timing | From 2 weeks | Strategy in 2–4 weeks, then the website | Strategy and brand in little over a month, then the website |

## What to keep

Decades of trust are an asset. Before changing anything, ask clients, sales and the team what they would miss: the name, a colour, a symbol, a phrase. What people recognise is worth protecting. What nobody notices can go.

## Signs it’s more than a refresh

- The company has gone from local to international.
- A new business line doesn’t fit the current story.
- There has been a merger, an acquisition or a new investor.
- Each salesperson explains the company differently.
- The website describes a company that no longer exists.

## The two mistakes

**Doing too little.** A new logo on top of an unclear story. The confusion stays, only better dressed.

**Doing too much.** Throwing away recognition you spent decades building, for the sake of looking new.

## FAQ

**Can we refresh now and rebrand later?** Yes, if the strategy is clear. A refresh without strategy usually ends up being redone.

**What if the founders want to keep everything?** That’s common in family businesses. The strategy listens to everyone — founders, the next generation and the team — and decisions are taken together.
